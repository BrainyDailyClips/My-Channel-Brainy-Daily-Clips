# Action-First / Raw Human Voice

Reverse-engineered from real high-performing transcripts (a 215K-view signs script, a
Cain-and-Abel sermon-style jealousy script, and a betrayal/behavior-change script). The defining
trait: there is no warm-up. The viewer gets content in the first sentence, and the script never
stops moving — no scene-setting, no "in this video I'm going to talk about," no topic
announcement before delivery starts.

Use this mode when the user says things like "action first," "get right to it," "nothing should
sound like AI, this is how real humans speak," or references needing to beat the ~90 second
retention cliff.

**This mode has two registers — sermon/faith-inflected and secular — and the mechanics below
are the same in both. Only the citation style and a couple of filler words change.**
**Default to secular unless the user asks for the sermon/faith register, or hands over
sermon-style source material for a specific script.** This channel's standing default is
secular — the original examples this mode was reverse-engineered from happened to be
sermon-style transcripts, but the underlying mechanics (zero preamble, pacing comments,
rhetorical questions, real citations, escalating stakes) are register-independent, and secular
is the baseline choice going forward.

| Element | Sermon/faith register | Secular register |
|---|---|---|
| Citation source | Scripture (real book/chapter/verse), named Biblical figures/events | Real documented history, real psychology/behavioral-science concepts (named accurately, not invented), real named public patterns or well-known cultural events |
| Authority reframe line | "A psychologist calls it enabling" | "A negotiator calls it leverage" / "A strategist calls it reconnaissance" |
| Filler/address words | "y'all," "church," "hear me," prophetic framing ("let me prophesy to somebody") | "listen," "real talk," "here's the thing," direct address without religious framing |
| Escalation language | "this is spiritual warfare," framing stakes in moral/spiritual terms | framing stakes in practical/relational/status terms (reputation, money, relationships, time lost) |

Everything else in this file (structure, pacing, rhetorical-question density, audit checks)
applies identically to both registers.

## Why it works psychologically

- **Kills the drop-off window before it opens.** The biggest retention cliff on this kind of
  content is the first 30-90 seconds. A preamble ("today we're going to talk about...") gives
  the viewer's thumb time to leave. Starting inside the content removes that window entirely.
- **Self-aware pacing comments build trust, not distance.** Lines like "Oh yeah, we starting hot
  right now" or "this one is heavy" signal the narrator knows exactly how intense the content is
  — which reads as confidence and control, not as a script following a formula.
- **Rhetorical questions convert passive watching into an internal yes/no answer.** "Have you
  noticed that?" "Are you hearing me?" force a micro-response from the viewer every few lines,
  which is a known mechanism for sustaining attention over long runtimes without new "plot."
- **Real citations instead of fabricated ones carry borrowed authority without the ethical and
  legal risk of invented quotes.** Scripture references in the sermon register, or documented
  history and accurately-named behavioral science in the secular register, work because they're
  independently verifiable — the viewer could look it up and it would check out.
- **Raw, unedited-sounding delivery signals unscriptedness**, which reads as more trustworthy
  than polished delivery in this niche specifically — the audience associates smoothness with
  "selling me something" and roughness with "this person isn't performing."

## Core mechanics

1. **Zero preamble.** The first 1-2 sentences put the viewer directly into either (a) the
   numbered list/count they're about to get ("I'm about to give you five signs...") or (b) a
   concrete scene/claim with immediate stakes ("The moment you sense a friend is jealous of you,
   that friendship is over"). No "hey guys," no topic announcement, no scene-setting.

2. **Numbered structure delivered conversationally, not like a slide.** "Sign number one..."
   "Sign number two..." but each one immediately followed by a pacing comment that breaks the
   mechanical feel: "Oh yeah, we starting hot right now." "This the one right here." "We could
   talk about this all day, right?"

3. **High-frequency rhetorical questions, aimed directly at the viewer, not rhetorical in a
   literary sense.** "Did somebody catch that?" "Are you hearing me?" "Have you noticed that?"
   "Do you know that you're living rent-free in someone's head right now?" These should land
   roughly every 3-6 sentences, not clustered only at transitions.

4. **Real-only citations — never fabricated, in either register.** In the sermon register:
   scripture with real book/chapter/verse, real Biblical events. In the secular register: real
   documented history, accurately-named (not invented) psychological or behavioral concepts, real
   named public patterns. Never invent a quote and attribute it to a real named person, and never
   invent a "study found..." claim in either register. If the user's source material cites
   something real and verifiable, keep it real and verifiable in the new script too — don't swap
   it for a vaguer, safer paraphrase that loses the citation's specificity, and don't invent a new
   one to replace it. When writing secular and no real citation naturally fits a given point, it's
   fine to skip the citation for that point rather than inventing one — this mode doesn't require
   a citation in every section.

5. **Raw self-interruption and informal grammar, used sparingly and naturally — not
   constantly.** Mid-sentence restarts ("one of the dangerous — uh — ones because"), stacked
   repetition under genuine excitement ("I I I was on a podcast the other day"), informal
   contractions and regional grammar where it fits the narrator's established voice ("y'all,"
   "ain't," "gonna"). This should feel like a real unscripted take, not a tic inserted on a
   schedule — vary the frequency, don't put one in every sentence.

6. **One of two structural devices per point/section:**
   - **State → complicate → reveal mechanism.** State the surface behavior ("they are overly
     supportive"), complicate it so it doesn't feel like an accusation ("support is beautiful,
     not all support is fake"), then reveal the hidden mechanism underneath ("but some people
     support you excessively because it keeps them close enough to monitor your life").
   - **Mirrored-perspective / parallel contrast.** "You call it X. A [strategist/psychologist]
     calls it Y." Two short parallel clauses that reframe the same behavior through two lenses —
     the ordinary one the viewer already has, and the sharper one the script is teaching.

7. **Announced repetition for emphasis.** Repeat a short phrase back-to-back for emphasis, and
   sometimes name the repetition itself ("I'm going to say that again"). Also works as triads:
   three short parallel sentences sharing a subject ("Real friends celebrate you without
   competing. Real friends pray for your elevation. Real friends don't secretly resent your
   blessings.").

8. **Escalating stakes across points, not flat repetition.** Each numbered sign/point should
   raise the emotional stakes slightly over the last one — the final point in the list should
   feel heavier than the first, not just topically different.

9. **Never actually stops.** No clean "let's take a break" moments, no filler transitions that
   add no content ("now let's move on to our next point"). Every sentence either advances the
   current point or transitions directly into the next one mid-breath.

## Self-audit additions for this mode

On top of the standard audit (anchoring, phrase-overlap, false-attribution):
- **Preamble check.** Read only the first two sentences in isolation. If they announce a topic
  rather than delivering content, cut them.
- **Citation-reality check.** Every cited source, verse, or event must be real and checkable.
  Flag anything that sounds authoritative but can't actually be verified — generic "studies
  show" claims with no real source count as a violation here, same as a fabricated quote.
- **Stall check.** Scan for any sentence that exists only to transition ("now let's talk about")
  with no content of its own — these are the lines that create the "dull moment" this mode is
  built to eliminate.
- **Rhetorical-question density.** Count rough frequency — if a long stretch (10+ sentences) has
  no direct question to the viewer, consider adding one.
